Entrants send exactly one word their ex would use to describe them. The funniest, most painfully honest word takes the whole reward.

The program writes one word to `/output/entry.txt`:

> Practice

## Why this word

Judged, most important first, on whether it is really one word an ex would say, whether it makes the judge laugh, whether it stings with recognition, whether it lands instantly, and whether it is relatable:

- **An ex would really say it:** "He was just practice." It is the word a former partner reaches for, not an insult invented for the contest.
- **Sharp turn:** the whole relationship is reframed in a single word — the rehearsal before the real thing.
- **Stings because it is true:** the entrant plainly knows it. No meanness, just the honest audit of a relationship that was a trial run.
- **Lands instantly:** no explanation needed, and no near-synonym keeps the edge — "training" is a gym, "rehearsal" is a play, "practice" is the relationship.
- **Relatable:** almost everyone has been somebody's practice run, or had one. It names something people have lived through.

Candidates were scored against the judge's brief in `main.py` (`Prototype`, `Intern`, `Warmup`, `Understudy`, `Draft`, `Archived` were all considered); `Practice` won on every criterion, and the program validates the pick against the gate — UTF-8, 1–24 characters, exactly one word — before and after writing it.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```
